definition: >-
  The cyclical variation in temperature and daylight duration across Earth's year, caused primarily by Earth's
  23.5° axial tilt relative to its orbital plane around the Sun, changing solar incidence angle and daylight length.
symbol: null

common_misconceptions:
  - Seasons are caused by Earth being significantly closer to the Sun in summer (Earth is actually closest to the Sun in January during Northern Hemisphere winter).
  - Both Northern and Southern hemispheres experience summer at the same time (hemispheres experience opposite seasons due to axial tilt orientation).
learning_objectives:
  - Explain how Earth's 23.5° axial tilt alters solar angle of incidence and daylight duration throughout the year.
  - Interpret solstices and equinoxes on orbital diagrams.
  - Disprove the distance misconception using orbital data (perihelion vs. aphelion distance differences).
real_world_applications:
  - Agricultural crop planting schedules and solar energy system tilt design.
  - Climate zone classification and architectural daylighting.

## Notes

Earth's axial tilt causes changes in solar intensity per unit surface area and daylight duration across the year.
