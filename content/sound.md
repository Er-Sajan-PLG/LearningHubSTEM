definition: >-
  A mechanical wave produced by vibrating objects, transmitted through a material medium (solid,
  liquid, or gas) as a longitudinal wave. Sound cannot travel through vacuum.
symbol: null
unit: null
equation: null
common_misconceptions:
  - Sound can travel through space (sound needs a medium; it cannot travel through vacuum).
  - "Sound travels faster than light (sound is much slower: ~343 m/s vs. 3×10⁸ m/s)."
  - Sound always travels at the same speed (speed varies with medium and temperature).
learning_objectives:
  - Explain that sound is produced by vibrations.
  - Describe how sound travels through different media.
  - Explain why sound cannot travel through vacuum.
  - Relate loudness to amplitude and pitch to frequency.
real_world_applications:
  - Musical instruments.
  - Ultrasound imaging.
  - SONAR (underwater detection).
  - Noise control and acoustics.
key_experiments:
  - Tuning fork in water (vibrations create waves).
  - Bell jar experiment (sound needs a medium).
