id: lhs:chem.alkene
type: concept
name: Alkene
domain: chemistry
status: draft
definition: >-
  An unsaturated hydrocarbon that contains at least one carbon-to-carbon double bond, with the
  general formula CₙH₂ₙ as a member of the homologous series. The presence of the double bond makes
  alkenes more reactive than alkanes.
symbol: null
unit: null
equation: "C_n H_{2n}  (acyclic alkenes with one double bond, n ≥ 2)"
examples:
  - "Ethene (C₂H₄) is the industrial precursor of polyethylene."
  - "Propene (C₃H₆) is a feedstock for polypropylene plastics."
key_experiments:
  - "Decolourise bromine water with an alkene to demonstrate addition across the double bond."
common_misconceptions:
  - Alkenes are stable and unreactive (the electron-rich double bond readily undergoes addition).
  - An alkene and its isomeric alkane have the same properties.
learning_objectives:
  - Identify the carbon-carbon double bond and write alkene names and formulas.
  - Explain addition reactions such as hydrogenation and hydration.
  - Relate the double bond to geometric (cis/trans) isomerism.
real_world_applications:
  - Manufacture of polymers such as polyethylene and polypropylene.
  - Ripening agents for fruit and feedstocks for alcohols.
