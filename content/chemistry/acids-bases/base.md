definition: >-
  A chemical substance that reacts with acids to form salt and water, accepts protons (H⁺), or releases hydroxide
  ions (OH⁻) in aqueous solution (alkali). Bases taste bitter, feel slippery, turn red litmus paper blue, and have a pH greater than 7.

common_misconceptions:
  - Bases are completely harmless compared to acids (strong alkalis like sodium hydroxide cause severe chemical burns).
  - All bases are soluble in water (alkalis are water-soluble bases; insoluble bases like copper oxide are not alkalis).
learning_objectives:
  - Define a base (proton acceptor / hydroxide producer) and distinguish alkalis from insoluble bases.
  - Explain strong vs. weak bases in terms of dissociation.
  - Describe neutralization reactions of bases with acids.
real_world_applications:
  - Antacids for neutralizing stomach acid.
  - Household cleaning products (ammonia, drain cleaner / sodium hydroxide).

## Notes

Bases act as proton acceptors in Brønsted-Lowry acid-base reactions.
